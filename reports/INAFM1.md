# Part I — Template A

## 0. Bench summary (for a bench biologist; plain words)

- **Claim:** INAFM1, a 142-aa single-pass membrane protein with no published function, behaves in CRISPR fitness data
  like a participant in polyubiquitin-dependent protein handling. The claim is **"INAFM1 functions in / supports
  polyubiquitin modification-dependent protein binding (GO:0031593)"** — not that it is a subunit of anything, and
  **not** that it is in any named compartment. *No evidence localises INAFM1 to the ER*: UniProt records only
  "Membrane … Single-pass" as a curator inference (ECO:0000305), HPA has no entry, and the one characterised family
  member (INAFM2/ROME) is a plasma-membrane protein. "ER–proteasome interface" appears in this report only as the
  **hypothesis to be tested**, never as a finding.
- **What carries the claim, stated up front.** Two disjoint sets of partners, and the reader should see both:
  the *label* GO:0031593 is generated by RAD23B, VCP and UBQLN1, whose **raw** Chronos correlations with INAFM1 are
  −0.009, +0.052 and −0.007 † — essentially zero (and VCP is common-essential, 99.9% of lines below −0.5, so it carries
  almost no usable variance). The *interesting biology* comes from the three strongest partners NFE2L1 (NRF1), NGLY1
  and DDI2 — the three components of NRF1 proteolytic activation — but those three are **one correlated axis, not three
  observations** (NGLY1–DDI2 r = 0.536, NFE2L1–DDI2 r = 0.475, NFE2L1–NGLY1 r = 0.436 †), they are **not** annotated to
  the predicted term, and INAFM1's attachment to that axis is weak (r = 0.117–0.214 †). p_cal is attached to the label,
  not to the axis.
- **Confidence:** **p_cal = 0.0367** [95% CI 0.016–0.060] = the expected probability that *the module term*
  (GO:0031593) is confirmed by D1-type experimental annotation within ~8 years. It is a hit probability for the term
  only; it says nothing about the relationship type, and nothing about the NRF1-axis reading in the paragraph above,
  which is an interpretation of the partner list rather than the calibrated object. Tier: none (p_cal fill; rank 37 of
  the primary top 40). No U gene reached Tier A or Tier B.
- **Strongest reason to doubt:** the prediction fails the lineage leave-out check — removing Skin lines changes the top
  term to a porphyrin/heme term and only 56% of the neighbourhood survives — and no orthogonal class supports it
  (physical: no support, p = 1.0000; co-abundance, localisation, perturbation phenotype and structure: not measured).
  The gene is dependency-flat (4 of 1361 lines below −0.5 in a panel whose SD is 0.133), so the whole signal comes from
  small covariation, and the two lines the experiment rests on sit 5–6 SD below an otherwise flat distribution — a
  pattern that is as consistent with screen outliers (clone effects, guide efficiency, normalisation) as with real
  dependency. Reproducing the dropout is therefore the first thing the experiment must do, and it may simply fail.
- **Prior prediction (post-review correction, 2026-10-31):** this is **not a new co-essentiality call**. Wainberg 2021
  (PMID:33859415, Supplementary Data 2) already placed INAFM1 (as PRR24) in a tight co-essential module with exactly
  the NRF1 axis — {DDI2, NFE2L1, NGLY1, PRR24} — and in a 12-gene module that also contains RAD23B and IPO9; its
  Supplementary Data 3 predicted "negative regulation of protein K63-linked ubiquitination" for it. What this report
  adds is the calibrated probability, the orthogonal-evidence and lineage checks (both negative), the literature check
  and the experiment plan, not the partner set.
- **First experiment:** knock out INAFM1 in two dependent lines with three guides plus a guide-resistant cDNA rescue and
  ask whether polyubiquitin-conjugate handling and NRF1 processing change beyond what a matched-essentiality control
  gene (**WAPL**, cohesin release — deliberately not a mitochondrial gene) does at the same growth effect; time
  ~10 weeks; approximate cost ~€35–45k (details in Part II).
- Cancer-line essentiality is not organismal function: dependency in DepMap lines says the gene is needed for
  proliferation there, not what it does in the organism.

## 2. The protein

- Domains (InterPro): **IPR029162 (InaF-motif)**; Pfam **PF15018**; no annotated functional domain (`Domain [FT]` empty).
  TM / signal peptide / MTS: **one transmembrane helix (aa 36–56, "Helical"), single-pass; no signal peptide, no transit
  peptide**. The rest of the 142-aa protein is disordered and low-complexity (UniProt regions 1–22, 61–83, 99–142; Gly-,
  Pro- and basic/acidic-biased stretches). AlphaFold pLDDT (global, AFDB v6): **59.5** — low, consistent with a short
  protein that is one helix plus disorder.
- Protein names (UniProt): "Putative transmembrane protein INAFM1 (InaF-motif-containing protein 1) (Proline-rich
  protein 24)". Length 142 aa. Protein existence: evidence at protein level. UniProt `FUNCTION` comment: **empty**.
  Subcellular location (UniProt, ECO:0000305 = inferred by curator): "Membrane; Single-pass membrane protein."
- Deepest orthologue: the InaF motif is shared with the *Drosophila* inaF locus proteins; PMID:17968007 states the motif
  is found in "organisms as diverse as nematodes, fish, and humans". Two motif lengths are quoted in the literature and
  they refer to different comparisons: **32 aa** is the block conserved *among the fly inaF-locus paralogues*
  ("a 32-aa block of residues that encompasses a transmembrane domain", PMID:17968007), whereas the *human–fly*
  homology for INAFM1/INAFM2 is "approximately 21 amino acids" (PMID:41650465). The human genome has two InaF-motif
  genes, INAFM1 and INAFM2; PMID:41650465 states that INAFM2 "arose earlier than INAFM1 evolutionarily". **No
  orthology-based function transfer is possible**: the *Drosophila* INAF proteins are TRP-channel auxiliary subunits
  (PMID:32434796), a function with no counterpart in the predicted module, and sharing a ~21-aa motif is not sharing a
  function. This no-paralogue-transfer rule is applied symmetrically throughout this report (see §4 on AKIRIN1).
- Class H (homology) evidence for the predicted term: **no IBA/ISO annotation for g** (bundle §6). The gene's only GO
  annotation of any kind in the current GAF is `INAFM1 located_in GO:0016020 GO_REF:0000044 IEA` — an electronic,
  CC-only membrane call.
- Source keys: UniProt C9JVW0; PMID:17968007; PMID:32434796; PMID:41650465; `reports/data/INAFM1/bundle.md` §8; GOA
  2026-05-21. All appear in Appendix A.

1. **The protein-family name is a false friend.** INAFM1 is named for a 32-aa transmembrane motif defined in

   organisms as diverse as nematodes, fish, and humans" [PMID:17968007]. The fly INAF proteins are TRP-channel

2. **The paralogue INAFM2 was characterised in 2026 and INAFM1 was explicitly not.** PMID:41650465 renames INAFM2

   not associated with growth dependence" in the lines where data existed — a reminder that family members can be
   functionally important and dependency-flat at the same time.

## 8. Alternative explanations

For each artefact check in §5, the evidence against it:

- **Chromosomal proximity / same-arm artefact:** excluded by construction; 0 of 10 partners share g's chromosome.
- **Paralogue cross-reactivity of guides:** INAFM1 has 0 paralogues in the matrix; the InaF-motif relative INAFM2 is not
  a sequence paralogue at the level Ensembl records here. Guide-level confirmation is nevertheless impossible in the
  data (no guide maps) and is pushed into the experiment (Part II §3).
- **Copy-number co-amplification:** r ≤ 0.05 for all three supporting partners.
- **Hub-driven call:** hub share of S is 0.00. NGLY1 is a hub (in-degree 73) but contributes to the neighbourhood, not
  to the term's S.
- **Lineage artefact: NOT excluded.** This is the live one. Skin lines are the most dependent lineage (mean −0.146,
  MW p = 0.0000) and removing them changes the top term entirely. A melanocyte/keratinocyte-specific covariation —
  for example shared sensitivity to proteotoxic or pigment-related stress — could generate the neighbourhood without
  INAFM1 participating in the pathway. The experiment must therefore be run in non-Skin lines (it is: Esophagus/Stomach
  and Prostate), which is a deliberate stress test of the prediction rather than a convenience.
- **Statistic-dependence:** the GLS version of the same neighbourhood gives an unrelated top term (lipid transport).
  The call is not robust to swapping the scoring statistic, and the report should not be read as if it were.
- **Expression-driven (type e) artefact:** ρ(effect, own expression) = −0.107 and only 1 TF among the partners — no
  support for a transcriptional-programme explanation.
- **Screen-outlier artefact: NOT excluded, and probably the single most likely reason Tier 1 fails.** The panel SD is
  0.133 and the two experimental lines sit at −0.79 and −0.94, i.e. 5–6 SD below the mean of an otherwise flat
  distribution, with only 4 such lines in 1361. For a flat gene, extreme single-line Chronos values are routinely
  clone effects, guide-efficiency outliers or normalisation artefacts. Nothing in the data distinguishes this from a
  real selective dependency, which is why the experiment's Tier-1 gate is dropout reproduction with three guides and a
  rescue, before any mechanism is read.
- **Guide cross-reactivity, with a free control:** the InaF-motif relative **INAFM2 is present in the DepMap matrix**
  (`INAFM2 (100505573)`; mean effect **+0.154**, SD 0.140, **0 lines below −0.5**, and r with INAFM1 = **−0.010** on
  the 225 lines where both are measured †). INAFM2 is thus a ready-made specificity control: guides against INAFM1
  should not produce an INAFM2-like (null) profile, and an INAFM2 knockout should produce no dropout in the chosen
  lines. This is used in Part II.

**Strongest competing biological hypothesis.** That INAFM1 is a **membrane-anchored supplier/client (type c)** rather
than a pathway component: a single-pass ER/membrane protein whose loss perturbs the membrane environment in which NRF1
is retrotranslocated and processed, producing co-dependency with NFE2L1/NGLY1/DDI2 without INAFM1 being part of the
machinery. A weaker but real alternative is that the dependency is a **generic proteostasis-sickness correlation**: the
four lines with an effect below −0.5 may simply be lines with narrow proteostasis margins, so any gene whose loss
nudges proteostasis co-varies with the NRF1 axis. The experiment that separates these is the module-specific readout
combined with the matched-essentiality control (Part II §6): a pathway member or a client should change NRF1 processing
specifically, whereas a generic-sickness correlate should move the readout no more than an unrelated gene matched for
growth effect.

## 9. Falsifying observations (written before §6 and §7 were read)

1. **INAFM1 knockout does not alter NRF1 (NFE2L1) processing or the proteasome bounce-back.** Specifically: in both
   dependent lines, after acute proteasome inhibition, the ratio of cleaved (~95–110 kDa) to full-length NRF1 and the
   induction of proteasome subunit transcripts (PSMA/PSMB panel) differ from the non-targeting control by no more than
   the matched-essentiality control gene does at the same growth effect. This would refute the module call in its
   mechanistic form.
2. **INAFM1 protein is not in a compartment where the module operates, and does not co-purify with any of it.**
   Specifically: endogenously tagged INAFM1 localises to the plasma membrane (or any compartment excluding ER /
   nuclear envelope / cytosolic face) in both dependent lines, and IP-MS/TurboID of tagged INAFM1 recovers no NRF1-axis
   or shuttle-factor protein (NFE2L1, NGLY1, DDI2, VCP, RAD23B, UBQLN1, proteasome subunits) above a tagged control.
   Combined with (1) this would make the whole GO:0031593 call untenable and would point back at the INAFM2/ROME-style
   plasma-membrane biology instead.

## 11. Novelty and external benchmarks

*Corrected 2026-10-31 (post-review): the earlier version said no catalogue made this prediction.*

Agreement with prior catalogues: **Wainberg 2021 made essentially the same co-essentiality call.** Its Supplementary
Data 2 places INAFM1 (as PRR24) in a d = 0.9 module {DDI2, NFE2L1, NGLY1, PRR24} and a d = 0.5 ubiquitin/
deubiquitination module containing five of our top-10 partners (NFE2L1, NGLY1, DDI2, RAD23B, IPO9); its Supplementary
Data 3 lists a predicted function, "negative regulation of protein K63-linked ubiquitination" (from a broader
41-gene module) (§7). Our label (GO:0031593 polyubiquitin modification-dependent protein binding) differs in wording
from Wainberg's GO term but sits in the same ubiquitin/proteasome space, and the partner set agrees. hu.MAP 3.0 does
not contain the protein; Replogle 2022 did not measure it; Predictomes contains only low-scoring pairs, none with a
partner; Kim 2019 does not list it.

What is new here: **not** the placement of INAFM1 next to NFE2L1, NGLY1 and DDI2 — that is a re-derivation of
Wainberg 2021's module. What this report adds is (i) a calibrated confirmation probability for the label, (ii) the
reproducibility, lineage leave-out and orthogonal-evidence checks (split-half R = 0.192, p = 0.0050; lineage
leave-out fails; no orthogonal support), (iii) a literature check confirming there is still no functional study, and
(iv) a decisive experiment. The honest accounting is:

- p_cal = 0.0367, i.e. the expected confirmation probability is about 1 in 27;
- the label GO:0031593 is generated by three partners whose raw co-dependency with INAFM1 is ≈ 0 (RAD23B −0.009,
  VCP +0.052, UBQLN1 −0.007 †), one of which (VCP) is common-essential;
- the three partners that do carry raw signal are **one axis** (r = 0.436–0.536 among themselves †), are near-hubs
  (98.3rd–99.4th in-degree percentile †), and are not in the term;
- the lineage leave-out fails, the GLS statistic gives an unrelated term, and the pipeline's own V1/V1′ label points
  at the nuclear pore instead;
- no orthogonal class supports it, and blinded judging returned 0 because there is nothing in the literature to judge
  against;
- the four "dependent" lines may be screen outliers, which Tier 1 is designed to find out first.

The contribution is therefore a calibrated, checked re-statement of a published co-essentiality module, with a cheap
decisive test — not a new function call — and the test may well fail at its first gate.

## 6. Experiment that separates the alternative explanation

- **Alternative:** INAFM1 is a generic proteostasis-margin correlate (or a membrane-environment supplier) rather than a

- **Experiment:** the Tier-1 gate (3 guides + rescue + INAFM2 arm) first, then the essential-gene confound control plus
  the type-c bypass rescue, run together — acute INAFM1 depletion vs **WAPL and HAUS1** depletion at matched growth

- **Readout if the prediction is right:** INAFM1 depletion shifts the poly-Ub/shuttle readout and blunts NRF1 cleavage
  and proteasome-subunit induction well beyond WAPL and HAUS1 at the same growth inhibition, without a proportional
  ISR signature, and the pre-cleaved NRF1 fragment rescues the transcriptional and growth phenotype.
- **Readout if the alternative is right:** INAFM1 moves the readouts no more than WAPL/HAUS1 (generic sickness); or it
  moves them in proportion to ATF4/DDIT3 (stress, not module); or the dropout never reproduces at the gate (screen
  outlier); or it blunts the readout but the pre-cleaved NRF1 fragment does not rescue (the dependency runs through

## 7. Decision rule (fixed in advance)

Because two different objects are on trial, the rule is stated per object.

**The prediction (GO:0031593, the thing p_cal covers):**
- **Supported:** dropout reproduced and rescued by guide-resistant cDNA **AND** the *term-level* readout concordant
  (poly-Ub conjugate burden or shuttle-factor engagement shifted beyond WAPL/HAUS1 at matched growth effect) **AND**
  localisation compatible with the term's compartments **AND** Tier-2 physical or epistatic support.
- **Refuted:** dropout reproduces and is rescued, but the term-level readout is unmoved in both dependent lines while
  the assay is shown to work in OVCAR8.
- **Ambiguous:** anything else.

**The interpretation (NRF1 axis, which p_cal does not cover), reported separately:**
- **Supported:** NRF1 cleavage / bounce-back blunted beyond the matched-essentiality controls, without a proportional
  ISR signature, and rescued by the pre-cleaved NRF1 fragment.
- **Refuted:** unchanged NRF1 processing in both dependent lines with a working assay in OVCAR8.

**Common refutation for both:** dropout not rescued by the cDNA (off-target), dropout never reproduced at the gate
(screen outlier), or incompatible localisation in both dependent lines. An ambiguous outcome is reported as such with a
proposed alternative relationship type (most likely c, with the bypass-rescue result deciding).

**Noted asymmetry (red-team point 6).** Falsifying observation §9.2 is weak on its co-purification half: for a 142-aa
single-pass protein with likely transient contacts, a negative TurboID/IP-MS result is close to uninformative, as §6
itself argues. The crisp content of §9.2 is the **localisation** half. Correspondingly, the "Supported" branch above
requires Tier-2 evidence that this report's own priors say is unlikely to appear — so the realistic best case for a
single round of work is "term-level readout concordant, Tier-2 pending", which must be reported as *ambiguous*, not as
support.
