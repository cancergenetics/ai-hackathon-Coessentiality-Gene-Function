## 0. Bench summary (for a bench biologist; plain words)

- **Claim:** *What the method predicted:* RBM34 functions in GO:0006354 "DNA-templated transcription elongation", with the module label "DNA-directed RNA polymerase I complex" (CPX-2386) — a call produced entirely by five RNA polymerase I subunits (POLR1A/B/E/G/H) among its co-essentiality partners. *What that most likely means at the bench:* RBM34 works in the nucleolar machine that makes ribosomal RNA; its other partners are large-subunit pre-rRNA assembly factors (PWP1, DDX31, RPF1, CEBPZ, TCOF1). *What the literature adds (not the method's output):* its budding-yeast orthologue Nop12 is a **maturation factor for the large (60S) pre-ribosome** [E1, E2, E3], not a subunit of Pol I, so the maturation reading is a transfer from the orthologue and should be credited to the literature rather than to the pipeline. Relationship type: the frozen script rule assigns **a** (stable subunit, because physical/co-abundance support exists); the evidence in this report fits **b** (assembly or maturation factor) better, and both readings are given in §1 and §8.
- **Confidence:** p_cal = **0.1027** (95% interval 0.050–0.188). This is the expected probability that **the predicted term itself** — GO:0006354 DNA-templated transcription elongation — is confirmed by experimental (D1-evidence) GO annotation within about eight years. It is *not* the probability that the biology above is right, and it does *not* cover the relationship type. Tier: none (the gene enters the top 40 through the p_cal fill); rank 5 of 40.
- **Strongest reason to doubt:** the *label* is probably wrong even if the *module* is right. GO already carries a phylogenetic (IBA) annotation of RBM34 to GO:0000463 "maturation of LSU-rRNA…" [E33], which is **not** a descendant of the predicted term [E34]; a future experimental annotation of the Nop12-like function would therefore score as a miss for GO:0006354. In addition, the prediction fails two top-40 robustness checks (DEMETER2 non-replication, TP53-status flag; §5), and the perturbation-phenotype class (Perturb-seq) gives no support (§6).
- **First experiment:** acute degron depletion of endogenous RBM34 in a dependent TP53-mutant line, read out by nascent-rRNA synthesis (Pol I run-on) **and** pre-rRNA processing (northern/pulse-chase), beside PWP1 depletion as the module comparator — this separates "Pol I transcription" from "60S maturation" in one experiment; time ≈ 10–14 weeks; approximate cost €25–35k (details in Appendix C).
- Cancer-line essentiality is not organismal function: dependency in DepMap lines says the gene is needed for proliferation there, not what it does in the organism.

**Honest reading of the two failures.**

*TP53.* RBM34 knockout is **less** damaging in TP53-mutant lines (median effect +0.035 higher). Recomputed genome-wide [E35], the median of this difference across 18,434 genes is −0.0007 (IQR −0.011 to +0.011) and the median of the per-line mean effect is the same in mutant and wild-type lines (−0.149 vs −0.1486), so this is not a screen-quality shift; RBM34 sits at the 94.5th percentile of the genome-wide distribution. Every top-10 partner shows the same direction, several much more strongly (DDX31 +0.133, WDR89 +0.078, RPF1 +0.075, PWP1 +0.071 †). That pattern is what the nucleolar-stress literature predicts: impaired ribosome biogenesis stabilises p53 through the 5S RNP–MDM2 axis [E23], and part of the fitness cost in p53-proficient lines is therefore p53-mediated arrest rather than a direct proliferation requirement. The flag is thus *informative about mechanism* rather than evidence of an artefact — but it does mean the dependency magnitude is partly a p53-surveillance phenotype, which is why the experiment uses TP53-mutant lines and why a p53-independent readout (pre-rRNA processing) is required [E24].

Three further facts belong here, two of which cut against the reading just given.

1. **The rank-1 partner is a published p53-arrest trigger.** The same abstract that supplies E16 continues: "Depletion of WDR89 leads to accumulation of p53 in the nucleus, p21 accumulation and G2/M cell cycle arrest 24 hours post-irradiation." [E16b] That is an alternative route to the same co-dependency which does not require the nucleolar-module reading: two genes whose loss both activates p53 will co-vary across lines that differ in p53 status. The same paper is itself a DepMap-correlation study — its title is "Dependency Map correlation analysis reveals WDR89 as a genome maintenance factor" [E16c] — so it is not an orthogonal confirmation of anything here, and §7's description of WDR89 as previously unstudied refers to ribosome biogenesis, not to DepMap analyses.
2. **p53-axis genes are inside the neighbourhood.** The report publishes the top 10 partners; in RBM34's unfiltered top 25 the p53-axis genes **RPS27L (rank 15, z 7.61) and MDM4 (rank 18, z 7.37)** also appear, with MDM2 at rank 32 and PPM1D at 30 [E37]. WDR89's own top 25 is more p53-loaded still: MDM4 rank 8 (10.99), PPM1D 11 (9.96), RPS27L 16 (8.06), MDM2 19 (7.54) [E37]. RPS27L is itself a 5S-RNP-adjacent, p53-induced ribosomal protein, so its presence is compatible with either reading — but the fact had to be stated.
3. **The module survives when the p53-proficient lines are removed.** Refitting the tool (published Config, unmodified) on the **902 TP53-mutant lines only** and re-scoring with the §3.1 exclusions [E38] gives a neighbourhood that is *more* Pol I-centric, not less — PWP1 16.32, POLR1E 15.90, POLR1H 14.39, POLR1G 13.71, DDX31 13.66, TCOF1 12.85, plus the SL1 subunits TAF1D 12.10, TAF1B 7.62, TAF1C 6.71 and TAF1A — while **RPS27L and MDM4 leave N(g) altogether** (only MDM2 remains, rank 23). The top term becomes GO:0006360 "transcription by RNA polymerase I", with GO:0006354 second. Two honest caveats: the top-term switch is a sample-size effect, not a p53 effect (a random 902-line refit, seed 0, gives the same switch [E38]); and the mutant-stratum neighbourhood overlap with the full data (0.60) is *below* all three random 902-line controls (0.72, 0.80, 0.88), so the p53-proficient lines do contribute to the neighbourhood even though removing them leaves the module intact. The mirror-image refit on the 459 wild-type lines is uninformative: its overlap (0.40) sits inside the random 459-line range (0.28, 0.28, 0.56) and none of those controls retains the top term either [E38].

*DEMETER2.* The RNAi replication check fails (0.041 vs 0.058). Two things limit what this can mean: (i) in the whole evaluated pool the check fails for **182 of 204** measured genes (`results/ledger/M6_SUMMARY.md`), so it rejects ~89% of predictions and has little discriminating power; (ii) RNAi knockdown is partial and seed-driven, and the DEMETER2 matrix covers 712 lines of which far fewer overlap each pair. Recomputed over all DEMETER2 lines †, the strongest pair is RBM34–PWP1 (r = 0.125, n = 593) — the very pair the yeast genetics pairs up [E2] — while POLR1G and POLR1H are absent from the matrix. So the failure is real and is reported as a failure, but it is weak evidence against the call.

**Unblinded synthesis.** The blinded step saw only human papers. The decisive literature is about the **orthologue**. Budding-yeast *NOP12* encodes a non-essential 60S ribosome assembly factor: its deletion inhibits 27SA₃ pre-rRNA processing while the A₃ factors remain bound and pre-rRNAs stay stable, and in vivo structure probing traces the defect to misfolding of 5.8S rRNA, with helix 5 not stably formed in the absence of Nop12 or Pwp1 [E2, E3, E4]. A current review of ribosome-biogenesis factors maps the pair explicitly: "Nop12 x RBM34 Important for 5.8S folding" [E1], and a yeast–human factor table lists "PWP1 Pwp1 Structural RBM34 Nop12 Structural" [E6]. A quantitative/XL-MS timeline of 60S maturation places Nop12-tagged particles with nucleoplasmic Nog2/Rix1 intermediates while noting additional enrichment for earlier nucleolar stages [E5]. On the human side, an independent proteogenomic network (FunMap) reports that "The network neighborhood of RBM34 was enriched for genes involved in rRNA processing" [E13]; RBM34 appears among MERS nsp3.2-specific interactors described as "a complex of rRNA processing factors" with DDX51, NOL9, NOL10, UTP14A, WDR36, URB1, PPAN and PDCD11 [E14]; and RBM34 was identified as a novel NUMA1/NS1-associated factor whose knockdown reduces infectious influenza A production [E15]. The human cancer phenotypes [E9, E10, E11] are compatible with a ribosome-biogenesis gene (proliferation, p53 axis) but do not test the module. Two of the top co-essentiality partners have fresh, independent support: PWP1 is a nucleolar regulator of Pol I-mediated rRNA transcription [E17], and WDR89 — the rank-1 partner, until now unstudied — was reported in October 2026 as a genome-maintenance factor that "physically interacts with a number of nucleolar proteins" and whose nucleolar intensity rises after DNA damage [E16].

**Closest prior publication and what co-essentiality adds.** The closest prior statements are the review tables that transfer the yeast Nop12 function to RBM34 by orthology [E1, E6] and GO's phylogenetic IBA annotation to GO:0000463 [E33]. Neither is experimental evidence in human cells. Co-essentiality adds: (i) a human-cell, perturbation-based dependency link to a specific module — Pol I plus pre-60S factors — measured in 1,361 lines; (ii) a concrete, novel set of partners for human RBM34 (WDR89, DDX31, PWP1), of which WDR89 has no prior ribosome-biogenesis literature at all; and (iii) a quantitative, calibrated statement with pre-specified artefact checks. What it does **not** add is a resolution between Pol I transcription and pre-rRNA maturation.

**Prior prediction lists containing this gene:** Wainberg 2021 — not found in the full text retrieved from Europe PMC (supplementary tables not searched) [E25]; hu.MAP 3.0 — present in one low-confidence complex with SPTY2D1 (project file); Replogle 2022 — RBM34 is perturbed in the K562 genome-wide library (it is measured in the Perturb-seq source, §6) but is not among the 1,973 rows of `annotated_embedding_coordinates.csv` (1,974 lines including the header) †; Predictomes — present, top pair PWP1 (§6).

**Post-freeze (after 2026-10-29) papers confirming the prediction:** none found as of 2026-10-30. The WDR89 paper [E16] was published 2026-10-08, i.e. before the freeze, and concerns the partner, not RBM34.

## 8. Alternative explanations

- *Proximity / same-locus.* Excluded by construction and by the locus flag (0 of 5 supporting partners); the only same-chromosome partner is 150.6 Mb away on the other arm.
- *Copy-number co-amplification.* All five supporting-partner correlations |r| ≤ 0.08.
- *Hub attraction.* Hub share 0.00; RBM34's own in-degree is 37 (not a hub).
- *Lineage.* Removing Kidney leaves the call unchanged (N(g) overlap 0.96); no TF enrichment; no dependency–expression coupling.
- *p53 confound.* Discussed in §5: real, directionally shared with the partners, and mechanistically expected for a ribosome-biogenesis gene [E23]; mitigated by using TP53-mutant lines and a p53-independent readout [E24].
- *RNAi non-replication.* Discussed in §5: fails, but the check fails for 182/204 measured pool genes.
- *p53-surveillance co-dependency block (named alternative, from the red-team review).* Instead of a nucleolar module whose members happen to signal through p53, the neighbourhood could be a block of genes whose loss activates p53 surveillance: RBM34's rank-1 partner WDR89 has exactly that published phenotype [E16b], p53-axis genes sit at ranks 15 and 18 of RBM34's own unfiltered neighbourhood and at ranks 8, 11, 16 and 19 of WDR89's [E37], and all ten top partners share the TP53 asymmetry. **Evidence against it:** removing every p53-proficient line and refitting leaves a sharper Pol I/SL1 neighbourhood and drops RPS27L and MDM4 out of N(g) [E38]; the co-abundance class points at a nucleolar protein set (NIFK, UTP15, HEATR1, PDCD11…) that has nothing to do with p53 signalling (§6); and the TP53 difference survives adjustment for RBM34's own expression [E41]. **What it would still explain:** part of the *magnitude* of every dependency in this block, and the WDR89 edge specifically. The decisive separation is experimental and is already in the plan — the pre-rRNA processing readout is p53-independent [E24] and the lines are TP53-mutant.
- *Pure correlation with a general "nucleolar size / proliferation" factor.* Possible: nucleolar genes form a large correlated block, and part of the signal could be a generic growth-rate axis. Against it: the specific supporting set is the five Pol I subunits, not nucleolar genes at large; the co-abundance evidence is a nucleolar-specific set (NIFK, UTP15, HEATR1, PDCD11…); and the split-half reproducibility (R = 0.480, p = 0.0050) is far above matched nulls.

**Strongest competing biological hypothesis.** That RBM34 is a *bona fide* Pol I transcription factor — a stable or quasi-stable partner of the polymerase (relationship type a or d in the Pol I module) — rather than a pre-60S maturation factor. Evidence for it: the only P-class edges are to POLR1E and POLR1G; the top term and the V1′ label are Pol I; TCOF1 and PWP1, both rDNA-transcription-linked [E17, E20], are in the neighbourhood. Evidence against it: the orthologue is a nonessential assembly factor acting on 5.8S folding after transcription [E2, E4]; the Human Cell Map SAINT edges come from a nucleolar interaction map where pre-ribosomal particles and Pol I co-purify, and RBM34's other SAINT interactors (FBL, NOP56, NIFK, EIF6, RPL31, RPS6) are ribosome-biogenesis and ribosomal proteins, not Pol I-specific; and Burke 2023 AF2 models none of the Pol I pairs above threshold. Experiment that separates them: Appendix C.6 (the decisive Tier-1 experiment), read as nascent 47S synthesis vs processing of 47S→32S→12S intermediates.

**The GLS failure, which the robustness table understates.** Scored on a GLS (covariance-whitened) neighbourhood instead of zavg_BEF, RBM34's top term is **GO:0006816 calcium ion transport** — not a shifted version of the call but an unrelated one. This matters more than a table cell suggests, for two reasons. First, GLS is not a weaker method on the hold-out: precision@1 zavg_BEF − GLS = **+0.006 (95% CI −0.005 to +0.017)**, a difference the data cannot distinguish from zero (`results/holdout_eval/SUMMARY.md`; GLS is worse on MRR, +0.016 (+0.005 to +0.027), and on terms ≤ 50 genes, +0.011 (+0.002 to +0.021)). A call that exists under one of two equally-performing scorers and not the other is, by that fact, fragile. Second, the GLS ranks in §4 show *where* it breaks: the nucleolar core survives decorrelation — WDR89 is GLS rank **1**, PWP1 **2**, DDX31 **3**, and PINX1 34, POLR1H 37, CEBPZ 46, POLR1G 75 remain within the first hundred — while the partners that actually generate the term collapse (POLR1E **434**, RPF1 **2,920**, TCOF1 **3,155**). The honest reading is that the *nucleolar association* is robust to the scoring choice but the *Pol I transcription label* is not: much of the Pol I signal is carried by the shared covariance structure that GLS removes, which is exactly what one expects if Pol I subunits and ribosome-biogenesis factors co-vary through a common 'nucleolar output' axis. This is an argument for stating the module as nucleolar rRNA production (§4) rather than as Pol I transcription, and it is a second reason — beside the GO-branch mismatch in §0 — to treat p_cal for GO:0006354 as optimistic.

A third, weaker possibility is that RBM34 acts on rDNA chromatin rather than on RNA; the two RRM domains and the nucleolar RNA-binding GO evidence make this less likely, and it is not separately tested here.

## 9. Falsifying observations (written from the prediction; see the process note above)

1. Acute depletion of RBM34 in two dependent lines leaves nascent rRNA synthesis **and** the full pre-rRNA processing profile (47S/45S, 32S, 12S, 7S/5.8S) unchanged at the time point where PWP1 depletion already shows a defect, while producing a comparable growth cost — **counting as falsifying only once the assay's minimum detectable effect is established**: the smallest shift in the 32S/(32S+12S) and 7S/32S ratios that the CAMLG replicate spread allows the experiment to detect must be stated and must be smaller than the shift PWP1 depletion produces, otherwise the null is uninformative rather than falsifying — this would place RBM34 outside the nucleolar rRNA-production module and favour the cancer-signalling readings [E9, E10].
2. Endogenously tagged RBM34 does not localise to the nucleolus in the dependent lines, or re-localises to the nucleoplasm/cytoplasm in a way incompatible with the module (the HPA/OpenCell/Human Cell Map calls would then be artefacts of overexpression or antibody cross-reactivity).
3. RBM34 depletion shows no epistasis with PWP1 or DDX31 depletion (fully additive growth and processing defects), and its interactome contains neither Pol I subunits nor pre-60S factors — this would refute both the subunit and the maturation-factor readings and leave only an indirect, lineage-level explanation.

- **What is new here.** Not the function itself: the Nop12 orthology is in the literature [E1, E6] and in GO as an IBA annotation [E33], so in substance this is closer to a rediscovery than to a novel call, and should be reported as such (the N2 class assigned blind reflects that agent L searched human-gene literature only). Open item (red-team R7): the UniProt nucleolar annotation derives from Scherl et al. 2002, which proposed ribosome-biogenesis roles for 31 uncharacterised nucleolar proteins [E36]; its full text is not open access, so whether RBM34/KIAA0117 is among those 31 could not be determined here. **Chosen readout:** pre-rRNA northern blot (5′ETS/ITS1/ITS2 probes) with ³²P pulse-chase, plus polysome profiling — the template's "Ribosome biogenesis" row. The assay's validity for this module and its independence from the p53 confound are documented for human cells by the systematic nucleolar screen of pre-rRNA processing factors [E24]. For the transcription-side arm the readout is nascent rRNA synthesis (5-EU pulse / run-on), which is the quantity a Pol I transcription factor must change [E17].

### C.6 Experiment that separates the alternative explanation

- **Alternative:** RBM34 is a transcription-side Pol I component rather than a pre-60S maturation factor.
- **Experiment:** the Tier-1 decisive experiment, read in two channels at the same time points.
- **Readout if the prediction (maturation factor) is right:** nascent 47S synthesis ≥ 80% of control at 6 h while ITS2-containing intermediates accumulate and 7S/5.8S falls; RBM34 co-migrates with pre-60S particles; interactome dominated by pre-60S factors.
- **Readout if the alternative is right:** nascent 47S synthesis drops ≥ 40% at 6 h with intermediate ratios unchanged; RBM34 co-fractionates with Pol I; interactome dominated by POLR1 subunits; AF-Multimer gives a Pol I interface.
- A third outcome — both channels affected — is expected if RBM34 acts at the transcription–processing interface (as TCOF1 does [E20]) and would be reported as such, not forced into one type.

### C.7 Decision rule (fixed in advance)

- **Supported:** dropout rescued by guide-resistant cDNA **AND** a module-specific readout (pre-rRNA processing or nascent synthesis) changed beyond the CAMLG control at matched growth effect **AND** nucleolar localisation of the endogenous tag **AND** Tier-2 physical or epistatic support (IP-MS enrichment of module partners at SAINT BFDR ≤ 0.01, or epistasis with PWP1).
- **Refuted:** dropout not rescued, or localisation incompatible with the nucleolus in both dependent lines.
- **Ambiguous:** anything else — reported as ambiguous with a proposed alternative relationship type (d in the Pol I module, or e if the phenotype tracks proliferation rate only).
