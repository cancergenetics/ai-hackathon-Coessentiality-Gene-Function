# Part 1. Template A

## 0. Bench summary (for a bench biologist; plain words)
- **Claim:** Across 1,361 cancer cell lines [M82], the lines that grow poorly without TM2D2 are the same lines that grow poorly without its paralogue TM2D1 and without three membrane-associated ubiquitin/quality-control proteins (JOSD1, AUP1, STUB1; also STUB1's adaptor CHIC2) [M53, M54]. The GO term the model assigns is "response to endoplasmic reticulum stress" (tied with the more specific "ERAD pathway") [Q9, Q11]. Claim, stated tentatively: **TM2D2 shares a fitness dependency with membrane-associated ubiquitin / protein-quality-control proteins; "response to ER stress" is the leading hypothesis for what that shared process is, not an established module name.** Relationship type unresolved (d). This is shared dependency, not a physical interaction — and the partners are not equally firm: on raw Chronos correlations across the 1,361 lines, AUP1 is TM2D2's rank-7 co-dependency (r 0.27), JOSD1 rank 20 (0.22), STUB1 rank 39 (0.20) and CHIC2 only rank 252 (0.14) [M89, M90]; the partners barely co-vary with each other (JOSD1–AUP1 0.07, AUP1–STUB1 0.12, JOSD1–STUB1 0.13; only STUB1–CHIC2 reaches 0.34) [M86], so this is a star around the TM2D genes rather than a tight module.
- **Confidence:** p_cal = 0.0503 (95% interval 0.016–0.098) [Q10] = expected probability (about 5%) that TM2D2 is later annotated to "response to endoplasmic reticulum stress" by experimental (D1-evidence) GO annotation within about 8 years [M65]. It is a hit probability for the module term only, not for the relationship type and not for "the gene's function is this module" [M66]. Tier: none (top 40 is filled by p_cal; no U gene can reach Tier B) [M67].
- **Strongest reason to doubt:** the ER-stress label rests on only two partners (AUP1 and STUB1) and is lost if either is removed [Q12]; the partner correlations do not replicate in RNAi (DEMETER2) [Q13]; no orthogonal evidence class supports it (§6); TM2D2's dependency does not co-vary with the core HRD1–SEL1L–UBE2G2 ERAD machinery, although AUP1's does [M62, M63]; and TM2D2 has no raw co-dependency with any UPR sensor or effector (HSPA5 −0.00, ERN1 −0.05, ATF6 −0.03, EIF2AK3 −0.03, XBP1 −0.09, ATF4 −0.03, DDIT3 0.02; all |r| ≤ 0.09) [M93]. TM2D1 and TM2D2 are one observation, not two (§8).
- **First experiment:** acute CRISPRi knockdown of TM2D2 in two TM2D2-dependent lines (YD-8, ETCC-016) and one non-dependent line (SNU-46), next to AUP1, JOSD1 and STUB1 knockdowns and a matched-essentiality control (MEN1), reading the unfolded-protein-response panel (BiP/HSPA5, CHOP/DDIT3, ATF4, spliced XBP1) and tunicamycin sensitivity; time about 6–8 weeks; approximate cost USD 10–15k (author's estimate, consumables only). Details in Part 2 (Template B) of this file.
- Cancer-line essentiality is not organismal function: dependency in DepMap lines says the gene is needed for proliferation there, not what it does in the organism. Loss-of-function TM2D variants are in fact *selected for* in human blood clones [M26] — the opposite sign to the cancer-line dependency.

### 7.3 Unblinded synthesis (shared with `reports/TM2D1.md`)
*What is known about TM2D2 itself.* Almost nothing: UniProt gives family membership and no function [Q1, Q42]. In a genome-wide phagocytosis screen in human U937 cells, TM2D1, TM2D2 and TM2D3 were among the strongest hits [M24], and TM2D2- and TM2D3-knockout cells cleared amyloid-β aggregates poorly [M25]. A Daphnia CRISPR knockout of the TM2D2 orthologue reduced an inducible morphological defence [Q5]. In UK Biobank blood, loss-of-function variants in all three TM2D genes associate with copy-neutral LOH clonal expansions in cis [M26]; TM2D3 is one of several loci at which inherited rare variants are targeted by acquired CN-LOH [M28]. The authors read this, with explicit hedging ("suggesting", "may"), as a proliferative advantage arising through dysregulation of NOTCH1 [M84]. Taken at face value it means that losing TM2D function *helps* blood clones expand — the opposite sign to the cancer-line dependency — but the mechanism the source proposes is Notch, not ER stress.

*The family acts as one unit.* The three human genes map 1:1 to fly *bisc* (TM2D1), *amrt* (TM2D2) and *amx* (TM2D3) [M8, Q37]. Each fly single knockout gives the same maternal-effect neurogenic (Notch) defect, and the triple null is no worse, so the three genes act together [M5, M6]. Human co-IP/MS data detect TM2D1–TM2D3 and TM2D2–TM2D3 interactions [M11]; hu.MAP 3.0 and Predictomes AF2 models agree (TM2D2–TM2D3 0.999 / SPOC 0.999) [M45, M50]. All three mouse knockouts are recessive embryonic lethal (IMPC, as cited) [M12]. This supports the co-dependency of TM2D1 with TM2D2 (zavg_BEF 31.70 [M53]; raw r 0.51, and 0.41–0.51 with TM2D3 [M61, M78]) but it also means the two reports describe **one** observation, not two.

*Where the family acts.* Fly work places the TM2D function at the γ-secretase cleavage step of Notch [M7] and in Notch receptor distribution by a double-pass transmembrane protein [M21, M22, M23], with *almondex* acting as a positive regulator of the Notch pathway at several developmental stages [M85]; human TM2D3 promotes Notch1 surface presentation [M13, M14], and the TM2D3 P155L variant, which abolishes rescue of *amx* phenotypes, is associated with late-onset Alzheimer's disease [M19, M20]. TM2D1 (BBP) binds amyloid-β and was described as one of a family of three proteins sharing this structure [M1, M2]. Most relevant to our prediction, the 2025 description of bi-allelic TM2D3 variants shows that TM2D3 is an ER protein [M15]; TM2D3-knockout cells and patient fibroblasts dysregulate the ER-stress markers ATF4, HSPA5 and DDIT3 [M16] and show ER swelling [M17], and the disorder is linked to exacerbated ER-stress sensitivity [M18]. UniProt now records the ER localisation [M44].

*The partners.* AUP1 binds the HRD1–SEL1L complex and is needed for ERAD of misfolded ER proteins [M29]; it sits on the ER and on lipid droplets [M30] and recruits and activates UBE2G2 at the ER membrane [M31]. STUB1 (CHIP) is an E3 ligase acting in ER protein quality control [M36]. CHIC2 is a palmitoylated vesicle/plasma-membrane protein [M41] that forms a complex with STUB1 controlling ubiquitination and surface levels of cytokine receptors [M37, M38], binds CHIP independently of chaperones and attenuates its ligase activity, and its knockout phenocopies CHIP knockout in some cell types [M39, M40] (bioRxiv preprint, not peer-reviewed). JOSD1 is a membrane-associated deubiquitinase that regulates membrane dynamics and endocytosis [M34] and protects cells from proteotoxic stress from the plasma membrane [M35]. ATP13A1 extracts mistargeted proteins from the ER [M42]; ZDHHC6 is an ER S-acyltransferase [M43].

*Verdict.* The literature **supports at family level, refines, and does not contradict** the prediction, and it supports the Notch reading at least as strongly. Support: the TM2D3 paralogue is an ER protein whose loss alters the ER-stress response [M15–M18]. Refinement: (i) the partners are membrane-associated ubiquitin/quality-control proteins, of which only AUP1 is a canonical ERAD factor, and TM2D2's dependency does not co-vary with the HRD1–SEL1L–UBE2G2 core even though AUP1's does [M62, M63] — so "ERAD pathway" (the tied, more specific term) is less likely than a broader membrane-protein-homeostasis process; (ii) the best-documented TM2D function is at the plasma membrane/γ-secretase step for Notch [M7, M14, M85], and the newest human genetics reads TM2D loss through NOTCH1 [M84] — a competing reading of equal weight (§8), which the DepMap data themselves support no better than ER stress (TM2D2 vs NOTCH1/2, PSEN1/2, NCSTN, APH1A, PSENEN, ADAM10: all |r| ≤ 0.09 [M94]). No study tested TM2D2 itself for ER stress. DepMap-derived evidence in the literature: none found (the phagocytosis, clonal-haematopoiesis and TM2D3 studies are independent of DepMap).

- Closest prior publication and what co-essentiality adds: Gabillard-Lefort et al. 2025 (PMID 40449487) [M15–M18] for the ER-stress link (TM2D3 only) and Salazar et al. 2021 (PMID 34905536) [M5–M7] for the family acting together. Co-essentiality adds (i) that human TM2D2 and TM2D1, not only TM2D3, share a fitness profile across 1,361 lines with AUP1, JOSD1 and the STUB1–CHIC2 pair, a link not found in the literature as of 2026-10-30; and (ii) a concrete, falsifiable prediction that TM2D2 loss perturbs the ER-stress response as TM2D3 loss does.
- Prior prediction lists containing this gene: Wainberg 2021 — not mentioned in the main text (PMC8763319) [M72]; supplementary module tables not checked (not available locally), so unknown. hu.MAP 3.0 — yes, with TM2D3 and with FADS1/UQCC1/UQCC2, not with the ER-stress module [Q7, Q8]. Replogle 2022 — TM2D2 perturbed in K562 GW, weak profile, no module call checked [Q20]. Predictomes — TM2D trio interfaces only [M50–M52].
- Post-freeze (after 2026-10-29) papers confirming the prediction: none found (Europe PMC search for TM2D1/TM2D2/TM2D3, first publication date 2026-06-01 onwards, 2026-10-30).

## 8. Alternative explanations
- **Paralogue artefact (TM2D1–TM2D2, zavg_BEF 31.70).** The built-in rule removes TM2D1 and TM2D3 from TM2D2's list [M53], so the term call cannot be driven by the paralogue itself. But the two lists are nearly identical (JOSD1, AUP1, STUB1, PCNX1, ZDHHC6, PCNX3, MMD, ATP13A1 are shared [M54]), so TM2D1's and TM2D2's predictions are one observation. This is carried through every module-level statement in this report and in `reports/TM2D1.md`: the two genes occupy two of the 40 primary slots and two blinded-judging rows, and the module claim should be counted once, not twice. A guide that cross-cuts both paralogues would inflate their mutual correlation, but it cannot create the shared correlation with AUP1, JOSD1 and STUB1 on other chromosomes. Per-guide data are not available; the guide off-target check in Part 2 must include TM2D1 and TM2D3.
- **Proximity, copy number, hub, lineage:** no flags (§5). Lineage: retained after removing Head and Neck.
- **RNAi non-replication (DEMETER2 fail):** the most serious data-level weakness for TM2D2; the CRISPR co-dependency could reflect a CRISPR-specific effect (e.g., guide-level behaviour) rather than biology. TP53: nominal p 0.0382 only.
- **Label artefact.** The ER-stress term is carried by the GO annotations of two multifunctional quality-control proteins (AUP1, STUB1) and is lost if either is removed [Q12]; the rank-1 partner JOSD1 is not in the term. A different label (e.g., protein ubiquitination) would describe the same partners equally well (top-5 terms in bundle §2).
- **Strongest competing biological hypothesis:** the TM2D complex and its partners form a *plasma-membrane / endosomal receptor-homeostasis* module (with Notch as the best-documented client) rather than an ER-stress module: TM2D3 controls Notch surface presentation [M14], *almondex* is a positive Notch regulator [M85], STUB1–CHIC2 control surface levels of cytokine receptors [M37, M38], JOSD1 acts at the plasma membrane on endocytosis [M34], and the 2026 human-genetics reading of TM2D loss is NOTCH1 dysregulation [M84]. The DepMap data separate neither hypothesis: TM2D2's raw correlation with NOTCH1/2, PSEN1/2, NCSTN, APH1A, PSENEN and ADAM10 is |r| ≤ 0.09 [M94], just as with the UPR genes (|r| ≤ 0.09) [M93] — so co-essentiality favours neither, and the earlier version of this report, which dismissed Notch using mean effects, used the wrong statistic. Experiment that separates them: Part 2 §6 (UPR panel versus surface-receptor levels after TM2D2 knockdown).
- **Supplier/client reading:** TM2D2 could be a client of the STUB1–CHIC2 or AUP1 machinery rather than a functional member; Tier-1 "partner protein levels after knockdown" (Part 2 §4) addresses this.

## 9. Falsifying observations
Written from the prediction (term, partners) before the unblinded literature search, but after the orthogonal matrix in the bundle had been seen (the bundle presents both together), so not fully blind.
1. Acute TM2D2 knockdown in YD-8 and ETCC-016 does not change BiP/HSPA5, CHOP/DDIT3, ATF4 or XBP1 splicing, nor tunicamycin sensitivity, beyond the matched-essentiality control (MEN1) at matched growth effect, while AUP1/JOSD1/STUB1 knockdowns do.
2. TM2D2 knockdown leaves ERAD substrate turnover (NHK-HA cycloheximide chase) unchanged.
3. Endogenously tagged TM2D2 shows no ER pool (plasma membrane/endosomes only) in both dependent lines.
4. Combined TM2D2 + AUP1 (or + JOSD1) knockdown gives an additive or synergistic growth defect (parallel pathways).
5. Guide-resistant TM2D2 cDNA does not rescue the growth defect (off-target or paralogue cross-cutting).

## 11. Novelty and external benchmarks
Agreement with prior catalogues: hu.MAP 3.0 and Predictomes agree with the TM2D family complex [M45, M50, Q7], which the co-essentiality network also shows (zavg_BEF 31.70), but neither places TM2D2 with AUP1, JOSD1, STUB1 or CHIC2 [M48, M52]. Wainberg 2021: not in the main text [M72]. Replogle 2022: no informative TM2D2 perturbation profile [Q20]. What is new here: a shared fitness dependency between the TM2D family and membrane-associated ubiquitin/quality-control proteins (AUP1, JOSD1, STUB1–CHIC2), and with it the testable claim that TM2D2 supports the ER-stress response, as TM2D3 does [M16]. Not found in the literature as of 2026-10-30.

## 12. Shared module analysis: TM2D1, TM2D2, CHIC2 (referenced by `reports/TM2D1.md`)
Figure: `reports/data/TM2D_module/fig_module.png` (network of the three genes and the union of their top-10 partners; heat map of zavg_BEF).

1. **Composition (one observation, not three).** All three genes get the same top term from overlapping partner lists; TM2D1 and TM2D2 are paralogues with raw r 0.515 (each other's rank-1 co-dependency) [M90], so the module is one observation that happens to occupy three top-40 rows. JOSD1, AUP1 and STUB1 are ranks 1–3 for both TM2D genes; STUB1 is CHIC2's rank-1 partner (z 16.7), and TM2D1, TM2D2 and TM2D3 are CHIC2's ranks 2–4 [M54, M55, M79, M59]. The network among the 16 nodes has 103 edges with zavg_BEF ≥ 3, of which 6 have physical support: TM2D1–TM2D3, TM2D2–TM2D3, TM2D1–PCNX3, TM2D2–PCNX3, PCNX1–TM2D3 and JOSD1–STUB1 [M56]. None links a TM2D protein to AUP1, JOSD1, STUB1 or CHIC2.
2. **Positive control inside the module.** The STUB1–CHIC2 co-dependency (CHIC2's top partner; raw r 0.34 [M64, M86]) rediscovers a published complex [M37–M40]. This shows that this neighbourhood carries real complex-level signal; it does not show that the TM2D–STUB1 link is physical. STUB1–CHIC2 is also the *only* partner–partner pair that co-varies appreciably: JOSD1–AUP1 0.07, AUP1–STUB1 0.12, JOSD1–STUB1 0.13 [M86]. The structure is therefore a star centred on the TM2D genes with one real edge among the partners, not a coherent complex.
3. **What the label rests on.** For all three genes the ER-stress term is carried by AUP1 and STUB1 (plus PTPN1 for CHIC2) [M80], and is lost when any one supporting partner is removed (LOPO 0/2, 0/2, 0/3) [Q12, M81; TM2D1: `reports/TM2D1.md` T14]. CHIC2's own call also fails lineage leave-out and DEMETER2 [M57], although its K562 Perturb-seq class supports the term [M58].
4. **Refinement from raw dependency correlations** (recomputed, 1,361 lines; descriptive). Full raw r and genome-wide rank for the term-supporting and adjacent partners [M87–M90]: TM2D2–AUP1 0.272 (rank 7), TM2D2–JOSD1 0.221 (20), TM2D2–STUB1 0.201 (39), TM2D2–CHIC2 0.136 (252); TM2D1–AUP1 0.242 (3), TM2D1–JOSD1 0.165 (19), **TM2D1–STUB1 0.104 (rank 234)**, TM2D1–CHIC2 0.095 (333). So of the two term-supporting partners only AUP1 is robust in the raw data for both genes, and the STUB1 link — the second of the two partners the whole label rests on — is weak for TM2D1 in raw correlation (rank 234) and in GLS (rank 127 [T15 in `reports/TM2D1.md`]). TM2D1, TM2D2 and TM2D3 correlate with each other (r 0.41–0.51) but not with UBE2G2, SEL1L or SYVN1 (|r| ≤ 0.03); AUP1, by contrast, correlates with UBE2G2, SEL1L and SYVN1 (0.39, 0.25, 0.34), which are themselves dependencies of differing strength (mean effects −0.321, −0.055, −0.458) [M61–M63, M77, M78]. Widening the ERAD core to DERL1, AMFR, FAF2, UBE2J1 and the HRD1 complex changes nothing (TM2D2 |r| ≤ 0.07 [M95]). So the TM2D–AUP1 link does not run through the canonical HRD1 ERAD core, and the most specific tied term (ERAD pathway) is the least supported reading. Neither does the TM2D dependency track the UPR itself (TM2D2 |r| ≤ 0.09 [M93]; TM2D1 |r| ≤ 0.08 [M91]) nor the γ-secretase/Notch genes (|r| ≤ 0.09 / 0.08 [M94, M92]). The best-supported reading is therefore deliberately weaker than the GO label: *the TM2D complex is needed in the same cell lines as a set of membrane-associated ubiquitin-regulating proteins (AUP1, JOSD1, STUB1–CHIC2), several of which act in ER or membrane protein quality control* [M29–M43]. "Response to ER stress" is the hypothesis this suggests and the experiments test; it is not what the data show.
5. **Sober statement of the module claim.** Shared dependency, one observation (two paralogues plus a weakly dependent third gene; counted once in any module-level statement), p_cal ≈ 0.05 each — on a method whose hold-out precision@1 is 0.030 and whose calibration slope is 0.819 (not poor) [M69, M68] — no orthogonal support for any TM2D gene, blinded literature judging unaddressed for all three [Q26, M60]. The strongest independent support is the TM2D3 ER-stress phenotype [M16–M18].
