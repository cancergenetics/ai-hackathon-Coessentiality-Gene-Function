## 0. Bench summary (for a bench biologist; plain words)

- **Claim:** C5orf34 — an uncharacterised 638-aa protein with no recognised domain beyond its own family — acts in **centriole duplication**. Its co-essentiality partners are, without exception, centriole proteins: SPICE1, CEP135, CEP295, WRAP73, PPP1R35, CEP120, CCP110, CCSAP, plus CCDC77, MIIP and TBC1D31/WDR67, which were shown in 2025 to form the **A–C linker** at the centriole's proximal end [F7]. The relationship type assigned by the frozen rule is **b** (assembly or maturation factor), i.e. the claim reads "required for assembly of the centriole", not "is a centriolar subunit" — no physical-interaction evidence supports a subunit claim.
- **Confidence:** p_cal = **0.0443** (95% interval 0.020–0.090). This is the expected probability that **the predicted term** GO:1902115 "regulation of organelle assembly" is confirmed by experimental (D1-evidence) GO annotation within about eight years. It is *not* the probability that the biology above is right, and it does *not* cover the relationship type. Tier: none (p_cal fill); rank 31 of 40.
- **Strongest reason to doubt:** the whole co-dependency is restricted to TP53-mutant lines — refitting on the 459 p53-proficient lines makes the centriolar neighbourhood disappear, where random 459-line refits keep it (§5) — and apart from the DepMap profile there is **no orthogonal support at all** — the physical class was measured and gives no support (one edge vs 0.43 expected), co-abundance, localisation and structure were not measured for this protein, and the perturbation class gives no support; the prediction fails both top-40 checks (DEMETER2 non-replication, TP53 flag); and the co-essentiality signal is carried by neighbourhood overlap (z_B 23.8) rather than by direct profile correlation (z_F 2.4; raw Chronos r with the ten top partners ranges from −0.01 for PCM1 to 0.21 for CCDC77, with SPICE1 at 0.06 †).
- **Prior prediction (post-review correction, 2026-10-31):** the centriole call is **not new**. Wainberg 2021 (PMID:33859415) Supplementary Data 3 already predicted "centriole elongation" for C5orf34, from a co-essential module that contains all ten of our top-10 partners. This report re-derives that prediction and adds the calibrated probability, the TP53-conditionality and RNAi checks (both against it), the orthogonal-evidence matrix (no support), the literature synthesis linking the partners to the 2025 A–C linker work, and the decisive experiment.
- **First experiment:** knock a fluorescent tag into the endogenous C5orf34 locus in a dependent line and ask, by expansion microscopy, whether the protein sits on the centriole (and where), while scoring centriole number after acute depletion beside SPICE1 depletion; time ≈ 12–16 weeks; approximate cost €30–40k (details in Appendix C).
- Cancer-line essentiality is not organismal function: dependency in DepMap lines says the gene is needed for proliferation there, not what it does in the organism.

**Honest reading of the two failures.**

*TP53.* C5orf34 knockout is **less** damaging in TP53-mutant lines (+0.035). Recomputed genome-wide [F15], the median of this difference over 18,434 genes is −0.0007 and there is no per-line screen-level shift (median of the per-line mean effect: −0.149 in mutant vs −0.1486 in wild-type lines), so the difference is real and sits at the 94.8th percentile — but it is small in absolute terms. Two biological readings are available and neither can be settled here. (i) C5orf34 is a direct target of the **p53–DREAM** repressor: it appears in the published list of 268 genes repressed through the p53–LIN37/DREAM pathway [F6], so its expression — and therefore any dependency on it — is itself p53-dependent. (ii) Loss of centrioles triggers a **USP28–53BP1–p53–p21** arrest [F10], so any true centriole-duplication gene is expected to be *less* essential where p53 is broken. Against both readings: the flag is **not shared with the partners** — recomputed †, SPICE1 gives +0.000 (p 0.83), WRAP73 −0.005, CEP135 +0.019, CEP44 +0.022, PCM1 −0.026; only CEP295 (+0.044) resembles C5orf34. If C5orf34 were simply a centriole-duplication gene, one would expect SPICE1 and CEP135 to show the same p53 dependence; they do not.

The red-team review asked for this to be resolved rather than noted. Two checks were run; together they move the question, and not in a comfortable direction.

**(i) The p53–DREAM/expression route is disfavoured.** If the TP53 asymmetry were a consequence of C5orf34's own p53–DREAM-controlled expression [F6] — the mechanism that would make this relationship type **e** — it should weaken when expression is accounted for. It does not: C5orf34's median expression is identical in TP53-mutant and wild-type lines (3.06 vs 3.06, MW p 0.39), the effect difference is present in every expression tertile (+0.048, +0.030, +0.028), and a rank regression of the effect on TP53 status is unchanged by adding own expression as a covariate (coefficient +96.0 → +95.3 rank units; p 1.1e-05 → 1.3e-05) [F30]. ρ(effect, expression) is 0.061. So the flag is not an expression artefact.

**(ii) The whole centriolar neighbourhood is restricted to TP53-mutant lines.** Refitting the tool (published Config, unmodified) on the **902 TP53-mutant lines only** and re-scoring with the §3.1 exclusions keeps the module intact: N(g) is CCDC77 12.66, SPICE1 12.20, WRAP73 12.04, MIIP 11.77, TBC1D31 11.67, CEP41 10.99, CEP44 9.54, PPP1R35 9.04, PCM1 9.00, CEP135 9.00, CEP295 8.76…, overlap with the full-data neighbourhood 0.60, and the top term stays GO:1902115 [F31]. Refitting on the **459 TP53-wild-type lines** instead, the neighbourhood collapses: overlap 0.12, only WRAP73, SPICE1 and TBC1D31 survive at low z, and the top term becomes GO:0007059 chromosome segregation [F31]. That could be mere sample size — except that three random 459-line refits (seeds 0–2) **retain the centriolar top term in all three cases**, with overlaps 0.52, 0.60 and 0.40 [F31]. The wild-type collapse is therefore not a power artefact: C5orf34's co-essentiality with centriole genes exists in p53-defective lines and is absent from p53-proficient ones.

**Interpretation, stated plainly.** The USP28–53BP1–p53–p21 reading [F10] predicted that a centriole-duplication gene is *less* essential where p53 is broken; the partners' failure to show that asymmetry argued against it, and the partner argument still stands. The p53–DREAM reading is now disfavoured by (i). What (ii) adds is a third possibility that neither the report nor the review had: the **co-dependency itself is conditional on TP53 status**. A gene that is co-essential with the centriole module only where the p53 surveillance pathway is disabled is consistent with a genuine centriolar function whose phenotype is masked (or overridden by arrest) in p53-proficient lines — and equally consistent with a p53-context-specific dependency that has nothing to do with centrioles. The data here cannot choose, so the question moves to the bench: the experiment plan now carries an **isogenic TP53 arm** (C.2, C.6) as a required rather than optional component, because the strongest single prediction this report can make is that a true centriolar phenotype must be visible in both backgrounds.

**Unblinded synthesis.** Three independent lines, none of them a direct test, now point the same way.

1. *Cell-cycle expression and p53–DREAM control.* C5orf34 is one of 701 genes whose expression tracks S/G2-M across human and mouse transcriptomes, and it is listed among the 205 putative cell-cycle genes that are "functionally uncharacterized" [F5]. In the same paper a subset of those orphans was screened for localisation by cDNA transfection — "we tested the subcellular localization of 28 candidate genes by cDNA transfection in HEK293T cells" [F5c], although the abstract says 27, a discrepancy internal to the paper — with the outcome "nuclear localization was the most frequently observed (15/28) followed by centrosomal-like localization (11/28) and cytosol (9/28)" [F5d] and only C3orf14, CCDC150 and C18orf54 confirmed against γ-tubulin. **Open item:** the identities of the 28 are not in the main text; they are in Supplementary Data S1 [F5e], which this pass did not download. If C5orf34 is one of the 11 with centrosomal-like staining, that is the strongest orthogonal datum in existence for this prediction and §6's "no orthogonal support" would have to be revised; if it is in the nuclear or cytosolic bin, that is a published localisation result pointing away from the centriole. Either way the answer is one supplementary file away and should be obtained before this report is used to justify bench work. Independently, C5orf34 appears in the published list of genes repressed through the p53–LIN37/DREAM pathway [F6]. A gene that is expressed in S/G2-M and switched off by p53-DREAM is exactly what a centriole-duplication factor looks like transcriptionally — and also what many other cell-cycle genes look like.
2. *The partners are a bona fide, recently solved module.* In 2025, CCDC77, WDR67 (= TBC1D31) and MIIP — ranks 3, 4 and 2 in C5orf34's neighbourhood — were shown by ultrastructure expansion microscopy to localise between microtubule triplets at the **A–C linker** and to form a complex whose depletion breaks the centriole's proximal end and impairs duplication [F7]. That paper found MIIP in the first place by the same kind of analysis used here: "we conducted a cross-DepMap analysis … Among the top 100 genes, we identified 12 that shared co-dependencies with CCDC77 and WDR67: WDR8, SPICE1, CEP135, TUBE1, TUBD1, TEDC2, RTTN (rotatin), CEP44, CEP295, PPP1R35, CEP152, and MIIP" [F7]. **Seven** of those twelve are in C5orf34's K = 25 neighbourhood — WRAP73 (= WDR8), SPICE1, CEP135, CEP44, CEP295, PPP1R35 and MIIP; TUBE1, TUBD1, TEDC2, RTTN and CEP152 are not (unfiltered ranks 74, 318, 365, 558 and 646). Because `results/ledger/*` publishes only the top 10 partners and the 8 supporting partners, this sentence is not reproducible from the shipped ledger; it is recomputed from `data/derived/matrices/zavg_full/zavg_BEF.npy` by `results/m7/scratch_reports_rbm34/nbr_ranks.py` (output `nbr_ranks.txt`), which also prints C5orf34's full unfiltered top 25 †. **And the reverse direction is sharper still:** C5orf34 is **rank 10 in CCDC77's own neighbourhood (z 12.59) and rank 10 in TBC1D31/WDR67's (z 12.35)** †, above PPP1R35, CCSAP, CEP120, TUBE1, TUBD1 and TEDC2 — several of which that paper did list and validate — yet the words C5orf34, CE034 and Q96MH7 appear nowhere in its text. One qualification keeps this from being a story about experts discarding a dark gene: the paper's criterion was "Common DepMap genes among the top 100 genes most correlated (**Pearson**) with WDR67 and CCDC77" [F27], and on raw Chronos Pearson correlation C5orf34 is rank **10** in the CCDC77 row but rank **4,812** in the TBC1D31 row († `extra_checks.py`), so it would not have survived the intersection of the two top-100 Pearson lists. Note carefully: because that discovery is itself DepMap-derived, under DESIGN §6.4 it counts as **unaddressed**, not as support, for the blinded judging — and the same caution applies to reading it as independent confirmation here. What it does establish is that this particular co-dependency neighbourhood has already yielded one correct, experimentally validated centriolar protein.
3. *SPICE1 and CEP135 define the step.* SPICE1 localises to centrioles throughout the cell cycle and its depletion "impairs centriole duplication and causes severe mitotic defects" [F8]; CEP120 and SPICE1 are required to localise CEP135 to procentrioles and control centriole length with CPAP [F9]. So the module C5orf34 is attached to is procentriole assembly/elongation, which is what the experiment in Appendix C tests.

Against all of this stands §6: no physical, co-abundance, localisation or structural evidence for C5orf34 itself, and three independent interaction resources that place it elsewhere.

**Closest prior publication and what co-essentiality adds.** The closest prior statements are that C5orf34 is a cell-cycle-regulated, p53-DREAM-repressed, functionally uncharacterised gene [F5, F6] whose knockdown slows cancer-cell proliferation [F1, F2]. **No publication placing C5orf34 at the centriole experimentally, or assigning it any molecular function, was found in the literature as of 2026-10-30.** *Corrected 2026-10-31:* the module assignment itself is not new — Wainberg 2021 predicted "centriole elongation" for C5orf34 from the same partners (next item). ## 8. Alternative explanations

- *Proximity / same-locus / copy number.* No partner on chromosome 5; 0 of 8 supporting partners carry a locus or CN flag (|r| ≤ 0.05).
- *Hub attraction.* Hub share 0.00; 0 hub partners.
- *Lineage.* Removing Eye leaves the top term unchanged, though N(g) overlap is 0.80 — one partner in five changes, the weakest neighbourhood stability of the genes reported in this pass. No TF enrichment; no dependency–expression coupling.
- *p53 confound, now a conditional dependency.* Discussed at length in §5. The expression/DREAM route is disfavoured [F30]; the USP28–53BP1 route is weakened by the partners not sharing the flag; and the new finding is that the centriolar neighbourhood itself exists only in the TP53-mutant lines [F31]. This is the most important single caveat in the report: a module assignment that holds in two thirds of the cell lines and vanishes in the other third is not yet a statement about centriole biology. The experiment addresses it with an isogenic TP53 arm rather than by argument.
- *RNAi non-replication.* Discussed in §5.
- *Weak-dependency statistics.* Only 18 of 1,361 lines reach Chronos ≤ −0.5 and the mean effect is −0.070; much of the neighbourhood evidence therefore rests on small effects measured well. The split-half check (R = 0.424 vs null max 0.184, p = 0.0050) says the neighbourhood is reproducible, not that the effect is large.
- *Calibration family.* p_cal was fitted in the "other" family because `centrosome_cilia_mitosis` has fewer than 30 H genes (`results/ledger/M6_SUMMARY.md`); the calibration therefore does not use any information about how well this method performs on centrosome modules. p_cal = 0.0443 should be read with that caveat.

**Strongest competing biological hypothesis.** That C5orf34 is a **cell-cycle-coexpressed gene whose dependency tracks proliferation and p53-DREAM status rather than centriole biology** — i.e. relationship type **e** in disguise. Its expression is S/G2-M-restricted [F5] and p53-DREAM-repressed [F6]; the DepMap profile of such genes can co-vary with other S/G2-M genes (which the centriole genes are) without any physical or pathway relationship. The discriminating observations are localisation and the centriole-count phenotype: a type-e gene has no reason to sit on the centriole or to produce a specific duplication failure. Appendix C.6 states the experiment.

A second competing hypothesis is **ciliogenesis rather than centriole duplication** (TBC1D31, CEP41, WRAP73 and PCM1 all carry cilium annotations). This is addressed by including a ciliation assay in the Tier-1 arm.

## 9. Falsifying observations (written from the prediction; see the process note above)

1. Endogenously tagged C5orf34 shows no centriolar or centriolar-satellite signal in two dependent lines by expansion microscopy **while a comparably low-abundance centriolar control knock-in (CEP44 or PPP1R35) is detected in the same pipeline, and the measured detection floor in molecules per centriole is below the level a genuine component would have to reach** — the module call would then rest on co-essentiality alone and should be downgraded to type e. Without that sensitivity control the negative is uninterpretable: C5orf34 median expression is log2(TPM+1) = 2.99 across lines and 3.07 in D1, and no validated antibody exists.
2. Acute depletion of C5orf34, titrated to the same growth cost as SPICE1 depletion, leaves centriole number, centriole length and the proximal-end markers (CEP135, CEP44, CEP295) unchanged in both dependent lines.
3. C5orf34 depletion is fully additive with SPICE1 or CEP135 depletion in centriole number and in growth (no epistasis), and tagged C5orf34 pulls down none of SPICE1, CEP135, CCDC77, MIIP or TBC1D31.

- The ciliogenesis arm separates the second alternative (cilium branch) from centriole duplication.

### C.7 Decision rule (fixed in advance)

- **Supported:** centriole-number or proximal-architecture defect beyond the PPME1 control at matched growth effect **AND** centriolar (or centriolar-satellite) localisation of the endogenous tag **AND** Tier-2 **epistatic or** physical support (epistasis with SPICE1/CCDC77, or IP-MS enrichment of the centriolar partners at SAINT BFDR ≤ 0.01).
  Two conjuncts of the original rule are deliberately demoted, because each can fail for reasons unrelated to the hypothesis. (i) **Dropout rescue** is now *supporting, not required*: the dependency is weak (Chronos −0.63/−0.66) and the growth arm may simply lack power (C.4); it is required only if the power condition in C.4 is met, and otherwise reported as uninformative. (ii) **IP-MS** is a disjunct with epistasis rather than a conjunct, because §6 already predicts it may fail: three interaction resources place C5orf34 with CSNK2B/FKBP5/DNAJC7/THBS3/RADX/TBCC and none with a centriolar protein, and a transient or sub-stoichiometric centriolar association would not survive a lysis-based pull-down. A negative IP-MS with a positive epistasis result is therefore "Supported"; a negative on both is not.
- **Refuted:** localisation incompatible with the centriole/satellite compartment in both dependent lines **while the abundance control is detected**, or a dropout that is reproducible and *not* rescued by guide-resistant cDNA (an off-target or proximity artefact).
- **Ambiguous:** anything else — reported as ambiguous with a proposed alternative relationship type (e, if the phenotype tracks proliferation and p53 status only; or a cilium-branch reassignment).
